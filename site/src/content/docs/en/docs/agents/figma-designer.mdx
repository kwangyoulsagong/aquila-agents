With Figma connected, your agents can read designs through Figma's official MCP server. Aquila adds two things on
top: **prompt chips** that appear when you paste a Figma link, and **Designer**, which shows the running app and
the Figma frame together so you can see what doesn't match.

## Connect Figma

Figma is turned on **per project**.

<Steps>

1. Press <kbd>⌘K</kbd> → **Connect Figma…** (also in the **Help** menu). It opens the project's settings at the
   **Figma MCP** block.

2. Switch it **On**.

3. Click **Log in from Claude** (or **Save and log in**). A new Claude tab opens with `/mcp` typed in. Press
   <kbd>Enter</kbd>, pick **figma**, then **Authenticate**, and finish the login in your browser.

4. Optional: click **Check connection**. It shows your Figma account and plan.

</Steps>

For Codex, log in once in a terminal:

```sh
codex mcp login figma
```

<Shot id="figma" caption="Figma MCP in project settings, and the chips a pasted link brings up." />

### What it changes

- New Claude and Codex tabs in the project get the Figma MCP server. Your own MCP servers stay as they are, and
  nothing is written to the repo or `~/.codex`.
- Tabs that are already open keep what they started with. Settings offers **Save and reopen** for them; the
  conversation carries on.
- The login lives in the CLIs (per Claude account), not in Aquila.

<Aside type="note" title="Figma plans">
  Some Figma features depend on your seat and plan: **Design to code** needs a Dev or Full seat, and **Code
  Connect** may need an Organization plan. Aquila shows these as hints next to your plan; it doesn't enforce limits.
  If the plan is unknown, see **Figma docs**.
</Aside>

## Turn a Figma link into a prompt

Paste, type or drop a Figma link into a Claude or Codex tab. A row of chips appears over the terminal:

| Group | Chip | Asks the agent to… |
|---|---|---|
| **Design** | **Compare with app** | compare the frame with the running app and fix spacing, color, type and size |
| **Design** | **Comments → TODOs** | turn the file's comments into a TODO list, marking backend work |
| **Build** | **Build with our components** | build the frame with your design system's components and tokens |
| **Build** | **Get assets** | download the frame's assets |

For a FigJam board you get **Flow → screens** (screens, states and backend TODOs) and **Comments → TODOs**.

A chip adds its prompt after the link in your input — it doesn't press Enter. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to send, or
dismiss the chips with **×**.

<Aside type="tip">
  In Claude tabs, **Compare with app** and **Build with our components** also ask Claude to check its work with
  Claude in Chrome: open the Figma frame and the running app, compare them layer by layer, and fix what the MCP data
  missed. Codex gets the MCP-only prompt.
</Aside>

## Compare the app with the design (Designer)

Designer lives in the [preview](/en/docs/agents/preview/) of a project with Figma on.

<Steps>

1. Open the preview (<kbd>⇧⌘P</kbd>) and click **Designer**.

2. Paste a Figma frame link into the **Figma link** field. The first time, log in to Figma inside the view; it
   stays logged in.

3. Pick a **Layout**: **Stacked**, **Side by side** or **Overlay**.

</Steps>

<Shot id="designer" caption="Overlay with Difference: only the pixels that differ stay bright." />

**Overlay** captures both views and lays the Figma frame over the app:

- Drag **Figma opacity** to fade between them.
- Turn on **Difference**: identical pixels turn black and only what differs stays bright.
- After the app changes, click ↻ (**Capture again**).

**Send to agent** pastes both screenshots and a request to fix the differences into the focused Claude or Codex
tab. As always, you press Enter.

<Aside type="tip">
  Use a Figma **prototype** link: it shows just the frame, without Figma's UI around it, so it lines up with the app
  best.
</Aside>

With a Figma link open in Designer, [Select element](/en/docs/agents/preview/#point-the-agent-at-an-element) also
adds the frame link and asks the agent to compare the picked element with the design.

Figma is available for local projects only.
